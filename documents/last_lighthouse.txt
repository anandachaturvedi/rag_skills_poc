Far beyond the northern edge of the mainland stood the island of Bellmare,

a narrow stretch of rock surrounded by cold, restless water. There were no

permanent residents there anymore, though fishermen occasionally anchored

near its eastern shore when storms made the journey home dangerous. At the

highest point of the island stood an old lighthouse built from pale stone,

its tower rising above the cliffs like a finger pointed toward the sky.

Mara had been the lighthouse keeper's daughter. She had spent most of her

childhood climbing the spiral staircase inside the tower, counting the

steps as she went and listening to her father explain how the great lamp

worked. Every evening at sunset, he would polish the glass, check the

mechanism, and light the enormous lantern that swept a narrow beam across

the sea.

When Mara was twelve, an electrical storm damaged the lighthouse generator.

The authorities eventually installed an automated beacon, and her father

left the island. Years passed, and Bellmare became little more than a

forgotten mark on nautical charts.

Mara returned when she was thirty-one.

She arrived on a small research boat carrying equipment, notebooks, and

several crates of supplies. Her university had received permission to study

the abandoned lighthouse before erosion made the island unsafe. The boat

left her at the old dock just after noon and promised to return in three

days.

The lighthouse looked smaller than she remembered.

Its white walls were streaked with salt, several windows were broken, and

thick vines had climbed almost halfway up the lower section. Inside, the

spiral staircase was coated with dust. Mara climbed slowly, stopping at

each landing to compare the building with the photographs she had brought.

At the top she found the lantern room.

The automated beacon installed decades earlier was still mounted near the

center, but it had stopped functioning long ago. Rust covered its metal

frame. Around it were the original glass panels, many of them cloudy with

age.

Mara spent the afternoon cataloguing everything she could find.

Behind an old cabinet she discovered a wooden box containing her father's

maintenance journals. The pages were brittle, but most of the handwriting

was still readable. She sat beside the lantern and began reading.

One entry caught her attention.

Her father had written about a strange light he had seen beyond the western

reef during a storm. He described it as a pale green glow that appeared

beneath the surface of the water. It moved slowly, disappeared, and then

returned several hundred meters away.

Mara assumed he had witnessed bioluminescent plankton.

The following night, however, she saw it herself.

A storm was building beyond the horizon. Clouds covered the moon, and the

sea below the cliffs had become almost completely black. Mara stood in the

lantern room checking the weather instruments when a faint green light

appeared in the water.

It moved beneath the waves.

She watched it travel along the western reef, vanishing whenever the waves

rose too high. Then it appeared directly below the lighthouse.

Mara grabbed her father's journal.

The final entry described exactly the same movement.

For several minutes she stood motionless, watching the glow circle the

island. Then the old lighthouse mechanism suddenly clicked.

The automated beacon, which had been silent for years, began turning.

Mara stepped backward in surprise.

The lamp was weak, but it produced a narrow beam that swept across the

water. Each time the beam passed over the western reef, the green glow

disappeared. When the beam moved away, it returned.

Mara realized that her father had not been recording a natural phenomenon

alone. He had been trying to understand why the old lighthouse seemed to

affect whatever lived beneath the water.

She stayed awake until dawn.

When the research boat returned the next morning, Mara brought the journals

with her. She did not tell the crew everything immediately. Instead, she

looked back at Bellmare as the boat moved away.

The lighthouse stood silent again.

But beneath the waves, far behind them, a faint green light followed the

boat for several minutes before slowly disappearing into the dark water.
